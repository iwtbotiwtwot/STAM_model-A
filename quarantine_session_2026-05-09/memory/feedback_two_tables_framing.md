When discussing Model-A, do not frame it as "explaining a tension," "resolving an anomaly," or having a "potential error" defined relative to LCDM (or any other framework).

**Why:** Model-A is being researched on its own terms. Framing it as "explaining LCDM's H_0 tension" or "resolving a deviation from LCDM" subtly cedes ground that LCDM is the ground truth Model-A must defer to. Per the team_STAM commitment, both are competing frameworks compared against the same data — neither is the reference for the other.

**How to apply:**
- Model-A's predictions go on one table (its own intrinsic distance, its own A_0, its own bridge term as the photon-A traversal contribution, its own CMB θ_⋆ structure, etc.).
- Other frameworks' predictions go on a separate table.
- Both are compared independently against observational data, not against each other.
- Phrasings to avoid: "STAM closes the H_0 tension," "STAM has tension with LCDM at X," "STAM's potential error vs LCDM."
- Phrasings to prefer: "Model-A predicts X. Observation is Y. Match is Z." Then on a separate row, "Other framework predicts X'." No "tension" language framed as Model-A's burden.
