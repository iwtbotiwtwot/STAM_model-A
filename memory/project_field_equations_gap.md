**F3 finding (2026-05-07):** Bold STAM evades Birkhoff's theorem because it is not vacuum Einstein gravity. But this evasion exposes that bold STAM **does not have specified field equations**. The metric ansatz `g_rr = 1/[(1-A)(1-A²)²]` is a postulate, not derived.

**The numerical evidence from F3:** the effective stress-energy required to source the bold-STAM metric under Einstein gravity has a sign change at A ≈ 0.44. Outer exterior (A < 0.44) has negative effective ρ; inner exterior has positive. If interpreted as ordinary matter, this would be a NEC violation in the outer region.

**The two honest interpretations of this:**

1. **Bold STAM is modified gravity** (f(R), scalar-tensor, or non-metric with A fundamental). The "effective ρ" is a redefinition that pretends bold STAM is Einstein gravity; in bold STAM's actual equations, no NEC violation occurs.

2. **The A field is quintessence-like** with real negative-pressure regions. Substantive physical claim.

Both interpretations evade Birkhoff. Both leave bold STAM owing field equations.

**The real research-agenda gap exposed:**

Bold STAM needs a specification of its actual field equations — what Lagrangian or set of equations *uniquely* picks out the metric `g_rr = 1/[(1-A)(1-A²)²]`. Candidates:

- **Scalar-tensor theory** with A as the scalar field, with action of the form
  `S = (1/16πG) ∫ d⁴x √-g [F(A) R + ω(A) (∂A)² - V(A)]`
  for some functions F, ω, V chosen to yield bold-STAM metric as static spherical solution.

- **f(R) or higher-curvature gravity** tuned so the static spherically symmetric solution matches the ansatz. Probably requires non-trivial f(R).

- **Non-metric theory** where A is fundamental and the metric is composite/derived from A and its gradients. This would be most STAM-native but is least-developed in the literature.

Until one of these is committed to and verified to give the bold-STAM metric as a unique solution, the framework's response to F3 is "the theorem doesn't apply (true), and we owe the underlying theory (true)."

**How to apply:**
- When listing bold-STAM weaknesses, include "no specified field equations" as an active research item.
- Don't claim bold STAM "satisfies modified gravity equations" without specifying which ones — that's overclaiming.
- The metric ansatz is justified by: (i) weak-field GR matching, (ii) infinite proper time at A=1 to give no-crossing, (iii) preserved thirds-of-A structure. These are the constraints that *should* be satisfied by whatever Lagrangian comes out, when found.
