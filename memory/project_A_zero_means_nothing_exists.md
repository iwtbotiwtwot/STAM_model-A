These are the same ontological reframe seen from two angles. Both reject the "constraint imposed on A" reading and replace it with "positive statement about what the manifold IS."

**The clean ontology:**
- Spacetime exists → A takes the baseline value A_0 = 1/(12π) everywhere the manifold extends.
- A is a property *of* the manifold, not a quantity *constrained by* the manifold.
- The relationship is: manifold existence → A_0 baseline. Not: A_0 floor → manifold existence allowed.
- A=0 doesn't happen because A is something the manifold has; where there is no manifold, there is no A to take any value.
- A=1 is the ceiling for the same reason flipped: it's the saturation level (universe ends there); A>1 doesn't happen because there's no manifold above it to host that value.

**What this is NOT:**
- A_0 is not a lower bound that the field A is constrained to respect.
- A_0 is not a wall A bounces off, nor an asymptote A approaches but cannot cross.
- "A < A_0 forbidden" is the wrong framing. "There is no place at A < A_0" is the right framing.
- Same applies to A=0 (which would be no-manifold) and A>1 (which would be beyond-saturation).

**Connection to ledger framing:**
The manifold's structural existence IS the resolved-A entries at density A_0 per Planck cell (the (4π × 3) decomposition). Spacetime existing and A having value A_0 are not two separate facts; they are the same fact viewed two ways. The manifold is the lattice of A_0 entries; A_0 is what the manifold looks like at baseline.

**Composition rule consequence:**
For a point matter source on a baseline-A_0 manifold, the composition is:

    A_total(r) = A_0 + (1 − A_0) × (Rs / r)

Matter's displacement adds to the baseline, scaled by the available range (1 − A_0) so that A_total at the source's horizon r = Rs lands exactly at the saturation ceiling A=1. The (1 − A_0) factor isn't a constraint imposed to keep A_total ≤ 1 — it's just what the geometry gives once you have a baseline of A_0 and a ceiling of 1. The horizon at Rs matches GR weak-field exactly. (See G21, 2026-05-11.)

**Language to use:**
- "A_0 is the baseline" — yes.
- "A is a result of spacetime existing" — yes.
- "Above the baseline, displacement maps onto the available range (1 − A_0)" — yes.
- "A_0 is the cosmic floor" — drift-prone; prefer baseline.
- "The floor constrains A from below" — wrong framing; A_0 is what A IS at unperturbed baseline.
- "A=0 is forbidden / impossible / not allowed" — wrong; "A=0 doesn't happen because no manifold there."

**Strong-field calculations:** Use A_total = A_0 + (1−A_0)(Rs/r), not A_total = A_0 + Rs/r. The composition matters; the rescaling makes the horizon land at GR's Rs as it should.
