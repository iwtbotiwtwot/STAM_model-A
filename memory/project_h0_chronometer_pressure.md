**Finding (G28 + G29, 2026-05-11 evening):**

Cosmic chronometers — the most model-independent H(z) probe — prefer H_0 ≈ 68 when their compilation is fit freely under any reasonable shape.

| Shape | Best-fit H_0 | χ²/dof at best-fit |
|---|---|---|
| LCDM (Ω_m = 0.315) | 68.35 | 0.484 |
| STAM closed-form (retired) | 68.42 | 1.549 |
| EdS (matter-only) | 49.07 | 1.683 |
| V_3 Scenario 1 (≡ LCDM-at-best-H0) | ≈ 68.35 | ≈ 0.484 |

**At H_0 = 73.04 (framework commitment), the V_3 fit to chronometers is χ²/N = 0.757 — statistically acceptable, not falsifying, but not the best fit available.** The free-fit H_0 sits at ~68 regardless of which shape we give the data.

## Why this is real pressure

The framework's H_0 = 73 story has been: SH0ES local distance ladder gives the true value; Planck's 67.4 is biased by LCDM-not-accounting-for-photon-A; chronometer agreement should follow from STAM's correct expansion shape.

But:
- Chronometers do NOT use distance modulus, so they don't carry the photon-A bias.
- Even given STAM's actual expansion shape (V_3 = LCDM-equivalent), chronometers want H_0 ≈ 68.
- So the "LCDM-bias gives Planck 67.4" story doesn't cover the chronometer result. Chronometers see ~68 directly, model-independent of LCDM.

The framework's H_0 = 73 commitment rides on SH0ES alone. Chronometers, BAO, and Planck all sit at ~67-68. That's three independent measurements vs one.

## Three readings of what's happening

**(a) H_0 is genuinely closer to 68.** SH0ES has unresolved systematics; the chronometer/BAO/CMB cluster at ~68 is the real value. The framework should consider relaxing the H_0 = 73 commitment. Under V_3 at H_0 = 67.4, χ²/N = 0.48 — excellent.

**(b) H_0 = 73 is correct, chronometers carry LCDM-flavored SPS systematics.** Possible but currently unrigorous. The Moresco group has investigated this and argues SPS systematics are internal to stellar physics, not cosmology. The argument that LCDM-shape and STAM-shape both pull to ~68 makes reading (b) harder — if SPS bias were tilting toward an LCDM-shaped cosmology, you'd expect STAM-shape fits to land different. They don't.

**(c) Different probes weight different aspects of expansion + clock structure.** The "tension" is real but doesn't resolve cleanly to either value. Multi-probe analysis with proper systematics modeling needed.

## Framework's current stance (2026-05-11 evening)

Hold H_0 = 73 on SH0ES grounds for now, but tag it explicitly as in mild tension with multiple model-independent probes (chronometers, BAO, Planck CMB).

The commitment is not falsified. χ²/N = 0.76 at H_0 = 73 is statistically acceptable. But it isn't preferred. The framework's H_0 commitment is more exposed than the prior cosmological-commitment memory suggested — it relies on one probe (SH0ES distance ladder) being right and three others being biased.

If SH0ES ever resolves down (e.g., new Cepheid recalibration), the framework's H_0 commitment may need to follow. If chronometers ever shift up (e.g., new SPS calibration shifts ages younger), the tension might ease.

## How to apply

- Don't claim "chronometers confirm STAM at H_0 = 73." That's wrong. Chronometers are statistically consistent with H_0 = 73 under V_3 shape, but they prefer H_0 ≈ 68.
- Don't claim "chronometers falsify STAM at H_0 = 73." That's also wrong. χ²/N = 0.76 is acceptable.
- Do claim: "STAM at H_0 = 73 is statistically consistent with chronometers, but the free-fit H_0 under STAM shape is ~68 — same as LCDM. Mild tension exists; framework holds 73 on SH0ES grounds."
- When discussing the framework's cosmological program: flag this as a known soft spot. Multiple probes pull toward 68. Single probe (SH0ES) supports 73. The framework's H_0 commitment is structurally a one-probe commitment.
- Watch for SH0ES updates, future chronometer compilations, and revised BAO with better systematics. Any of these could shift the picture.
