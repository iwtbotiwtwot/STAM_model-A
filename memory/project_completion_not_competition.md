**Author's framing (2026-05-07, "burp 5"):** Model-A and GR align throughout the approach to a horizon. As time slows for the traveler and |∇A| grows, the two theories track each other almost perfectly — right up until A = 1, where the Model-A travel becomes infinite while GR continues into an interior that Model-A denies exists.

**Why this is the right characterization:**

The deviation factor between Model-A and GR is `(1-A²)² = 1 - 2A² + A⁴`. This:
- Equals 1 at A = 0 (no deviation, perfect GR alignment)
- Stays within ~1% of 1 for A < 0.05 (everywhere we currently measure)
- Only crashes to zero at A = 1 itself

So Model-A is *GR everywhere outside the boundary, plus a different ontology at the boundary*. Not a competing theory of gravity — a completion.

**Implication 1: Model-A doesn't need to "beat" GR.**
Every test of GR in regimes we can probe (solar system, pulsars, GPS) is automatically passed by Model-A. The framework's distinctive content lives at the boundary, where measurements are hard or impossible.

**Implication 2: the equivalence principle is preserved automatically.**
Model-A's metric is smooth and regular throughout A < 1, so EP holds locally everywhere in the manifold. At A = 1 itself, EP is moot because A = 1 is the edge — not a regular point. EP at the edge is like asking "is a beach wet underwater" at the shoreline.

**Implication 3: this is how to describe Model-A to skeptics.**
"Model-A is general relativity, with a different ontology at the horizon." Not "a modification of GR" or "a competing theory" — those framings invite arguments about precision tests Model-A doesn't aim to address. The right framing is *completion*: Model-A and GR are identical for the questions GR was designed to answer; Model-A also answers the question GR was never designed to answer (what is at A = 1).

**How to apply:**
- When introducing Model-A, lead with "GR plus boundary structure," not "modified gravity."
- For weak-field / GR-precision-test objections: defer to (1-A²)² being second-order.
- For boundary / no-crossing / no-interior content: that's the unique Model-A contribution.
- Equivalence principle, Birkhoff's theorem, and other GR-foundational concerns are not really attacks on Model-A — they're attacks on a *different* theory that Model-A doesn't try to be.
