**Author's framing (2026-05-07, "burp 5"):** Bold STAM and GR align throughout the approach to a horizon. As time slows for the traveler and |∇A| grows, the two theories track each other almost perfectly — right up until A = 1, where the bold-STAM travel becomes infinite while GR continues into an interior that bold STAM denies exists.

**Why this is the right characterization:**

The deviation factor between bold STAM and GR is `(1-A²)² = 1 - 2A² + A⁴`. This:
- Equals 1 at A = 0 (no deviation, perfect GR alignment)
- Stays within ~1% of 1 for A < 0.05 (everywhere we currently measure)
- Only crashes to zero at A = 1 itself

So bold STAM is *GR everywhere outside the boundary, plus a different ontology at the boundary*. Not a competing theory of gravity — a completion.

**Implication 1: bold STAM doesn't need to "beat" GR.**
Every test of GR in regimes we can probe (solar system, pulsars, GPS) is automatically passed by bold STAM. The framework's distinctive content lives at the boundary, where measurements are hard or impossible.

**Implication 2: the equivalence principle is preserved automatically.**
Bold STAM's metric is smooth and regular throughout A < 1, so EP holds locally everywhere in the manifold. At A = 1 itself, EP is moot because A = 1 is the edge — not a regular point. EP at the edge is like asking "is a beach wet underwater" at the shoreline.

**Implication 3: this is how to describe bold STAM to skeptics.**
"Bold STAM is general relativity, with a different ontology at the horizon." Not "a modification of GR" or "a competing theory" — those framings invite arguments about precision tests bold STAM doesn't aim to address. The right framing is *completion*: bold STAM and GR are identical for the questions GR was designed to answer; bold STAM also answers the question GR was never designed to answer (what is at A = 1).

**How to apply:**
- When introducing bold STAM, lead with "GR plus boundary structure," not "modified gravity."
- For weak-field / GR-precision-test objections: defer to (1-A²)² being second-order.
- For boundary / no-crossing / no-interior content: that's the unique bold-STAM contribution.
- Equivalence principle, Birkhoff's theorem, and other GR-foundational concerns are not really attacks on bold STAM — they're attacks on a *different* theory that bold STAM doesn't try to be.
