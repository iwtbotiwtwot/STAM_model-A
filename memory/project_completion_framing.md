**The framing in conversation:**

When discussing STAM:
- LEAD with: "STAM matches every observed prediction of GR exactly outside Rs."
- FOLLOW with: "Where it departs is at and beyond Rs, where GR's predictions are singular or undefined and STAM gives a finite, well-defined answer."
- AVOID: "STAM modifies GR" — it does not modify the equations or predictions outside Rs.
- AVOID: "STAM disagrees with GR" — it agrees up to the boundary, then continues where GR stops.

**Implication for empirical case:**

Every observation that confirms GR confirms STAM. The empirical evidence for relativistic gravity (a century of solar-system tests, binary pulsar timing, LIGO/Virgo events, EHT imaging, GRAVITY measurements) is *automatically* the empirical evidence for bold STAM. STAM does not need to win these battles separately — it inherits them.

The remaining tests are *theoretical* (information paradox resolution, singularity avoidance, holographic structure derivation), and on each STAM provides a cleaner answer than GR.

**How to apply:**

1. When asked "what evidence supports STAM": cite GR's confirmed predictions, then note STAM matches all of them.
2. When asked "what does STAM uniquely predict": cite the boundary ontology consequences (no firewall, info on 2D surface, no singularity, holographic structure automatic).
3. When asked "is STAM falsifiable": yes — any observation of physics inside Rs (e.g., a probe of trans-horizon structure, a violation of holographic bounds) would falsify it. Any disagreement with GR outside Rs would also falsify it (so far, no such disagreement has been observed).
4. Don't claim STAM "competes with" or "replaces" GR. Claim it completes GR at the boundary that GR cannot self-consistently describe.
