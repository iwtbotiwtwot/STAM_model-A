## The intuition, in Sean's words

> "If I watch 2 objects circle each other faster and faster they are warping spacetime around them, by the time they touch they have already formed a sort of 'doughnut' of space around them, then the final interaction pushes out on the doughnut. This could be the cause of light showing up behind the GW."

## What the picture says

- Binary inspiral (BNS, BBH, etc.): two compact objects orbiting each other at increasing frequency.
- The orbital motion warps spacetime around the binary system — building up a region of locally-elevated A around the orbital plane.
- By the time the objects touch (or merge), this elevated-A region forms a "doughnut" — torus-shaped — surrounding the merger zone.
- When merger occurs, the GW propagates outward at c through this geometry. The doughnut affects metric perturbations less than it affects light, because GW is the metric perturbation itself.
- Light produced at the merger site (gamma-ray burst, kilonova, jet) has to traverse the doughnut on its way out. The traversal through elevated-A produces a delay.
- The arrival-time difference (GW arrives first, light arrives behind) becomes a local source-region effect, not a cosmic propagation effect.

## Why this is interesting

- **It avoids F5c's falsification.** F5c killed Model-B's asymmetric-propagation idea by computing the cumulative delay across 40 Mpc of cosmic distance — got ~3 years vs. observed 1.74 s. The doughnut idea is LOCAL — only the merger region's elevated-A contributes — so the predicted delay is much smaller. 1.74 s for GW170817 might match.
- **It provides a framework-native explanation for the observed GW/light delay** without abandoning "both messengers travel at c through unperturbed space" (which is what survived F5c).
- **The mechanism is concrete:** local A enhancement from inspiral → SU/A traversal at the source. Same SU mechanism used elsewhere in the framework (G7 bridge term, G11 CMB).

## What's still open

- **Quantitative prediction:** what's the expected delay for GW170817? Would need to model the doughnut's A profile (depth, extent) and integrate SU traversal for light escaping the merger region. Compare to 1.74 s observation.
- **Mechanism by which GW "doesn't see" the doughnut:** under standard reading, GW propagation is governed by the same metric that affects light propagation. The "doughnut affects light differently than GW" claim needs careful articulation. Possible reading: GW IS the metric perturbation, traveling at c locally; light is a separate field traveling on the metric, affected by accumulated A along its path.
- **Generalization to other multimessenger events:** if this is right, the delay should depend on the merger's pre-merger orbital parameters (faster inspiral = bigger doughnut = longer delay). Testable in principle if multiple BNS mergers have multimessenger data.
