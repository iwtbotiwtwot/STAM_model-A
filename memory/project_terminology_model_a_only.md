**Sean's terminology commitment (2026-05-08):**

> "If they are the same lets stick with model-a. Burn that into memory please."

**Why:** During the strong-field development, "bold STAM" was a useful label to distinguish a STAM-native commitment (modified g_rr = 1/[(1-A)(1-A²)²], A=1 is the universe's edge, no interior, symmetric no-crossing infall) from a more conservative read that might have stuck with GR Schwarzschild outside Rs and only modified the ontology. Once the strong-field direction settled on the STAM-native version, there's no remaining alternative to contrast against. Carrying the "bold" label invites the question "as opposed to what?" — and the answer is "as opposed to nothing; this is the only Model-A there is."

**How to apply:**

1. **Use "Model-A" everywhere.** Drop "bold STAM" / "bold Model-A" / "bold-STAM" from new writing. The strong-field metric, boundary ontology, no-crossing infall, and Hawking-from-unresolved-A are all Model-A commitments — not "bold" extensions of a more conservative core.

2. **When editing existing memory or docs**, replace "bold STAM" with "Model-A" or "STAM Model-A" depending on context. The references all point to the same framework.

3. **Model-B (Framework D) was a refinement to Model-A**, but the asymmetric-propagation piece was falsified by F5c (see project_F5c_asymmetric_propagation_falsified.md). What survives from Model-B is the recognition that GWs inherit tensor character from being metric perturbations — that's just consistent with Model-A's metric having its usual tensor structure, not a separate framework. So Model-B's name is not load-bearing either; the surviving content is part of Model-A.

4. **The strong-field metric is genuinely STAM-native, not GR-borrowed.** g_tt = -(1-A)c² has h(A) = (1-A) which matches GR's form, but g_rr = 1/[(1-A)(1-A²)²] is the STAM-derived modification. The (1-A²)² factor comes from three Model-A principles working together: weak-field GR recovery at first order (so all GPS/Shapiro/lensing tests pass), A=1 as the universe's edge (so SU/proper-time integrals diverge there), and thirds-of-A preservation (which depends on g_tt only). The k(A) modification is Model-A's commitment, not a copy.

5. **Do not call Model-A "modified gravity" either.** Modified gravity suggests a different theory of gravitational dynamics; Model-A matches GR's predictions wherever GR has been tested (the deviation factor (1-A²)² is within 1% of unity for A < 0.05, which covers all measured regimes). Model-A's distinctive content is at the boundary A=1, in the boundary ontology, in the thermodynamic mechanism, and in the cosmological coupling V(A) = β/(1-A). "Completion of GR at A=1" is the right framing.
